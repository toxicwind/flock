
# FREDERICK VON MIERERS: ARCHIVAL DEEP DIVE
## The Real Story Behind Eternal Values — Not the HBO Version

---

### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Frederick von Mierers (born Freddie Miers, December 25, 1946 – February 4, 1990) was the leader of Eternal Values, a New Age doomsday cult active from the late 1970s until its final dissolution in 1999. He is the subject of HBO's 2026 docuseries *Bring Me the Beauties: A Model Cult* — **NOT** NXIVM's *The Vow*. Von Mierers has no connection to Keith Raniere or NXIVM. This report compiles primary sources, court-adjacent records, and archival journalism to reconstruct the actual history stripped of documentary dramatization.

---

### I. ORIGINS & IDENTITY FABRICATION

**Birth Name:** Freddie Miers (also reported as Fred Meyers, Freddie Myers, Frederick Meyer)
**Born:** December 25, 1946, Brooklyn, New York
**Died:** February 4, 1990, Lake Lure, North Carolina (age 43)
**Cause of Death:** AIDS-related complications

Von Mierers was born to a Jewish family in Brooklyn, raised by a single mother and his aunt. His father was reportedly a dry cleaner. He briefly worked as a model in the 1960s before reinventing himself as "Frederick von Mierers" — a name suggesting European aristocracy. He falsely claimed to be listed in the Social Register, invented ties to the Vanderbilt family, and claimed inheritance from a godmother in the Kress family fortune. None of this was true.

According to Hoyt Richards, von Mierers was born to parents who were 16 years old, shuffled to grandparents, and never developed normal attachment capacity: "Clearly, he had no issue with sex. But as far as real intimacy and connection, I don't know if he ever had it."

Those who knew his real background would shout "Freddie Myers!" across the floor at Studio 54 just to watch him flinch.

### II. THE ARCTURIAN DOCTRINE

In January 1978, von Mierers claimed a "bewildering substitution of egos" occurred alone in his New York bedroom. He told journalist Ruth Montgomery:

> "During the next seven days, three beings materialized before me in my room, and deep secrets were revealed to me... Overnight I became an adept in the science of Hindu astrology. I knew that I had come from Arcturus, where I had lived in a hydrogen-light body, and I knew why I had come back... In a series of visions, I saw the coming wars, the destruction of New York City, my own mission, and the future of the Earth."

His claimed mission: reach "wholesome" young people to prepare them to lead a new society after Earth's "pole shift" around 2000. He preached that Arcturian "walk-ins" (alien energies inhabiting human bodies) would be rescued by UFOs while the rest of humanity perished.

Ruth Montgomery's 1985 bestseller *Aliens Among Us* gave von Mierers critical legitimacy, confirming him as an Arcturian "walk-in." A Ford model later told Vanity Fair: "Ruth Montgomery really created Frederick."

The doctrine evolved to include:
- Strict celibacy and ego renunciation
- Restrictive diets (fruits/vegetables, no processed foods)
- Regular tanning sessions
- "Slamming sessions" — group verbal abuse for rule-breakers
- Mandatory deep-cleaning of von Mierers' apartment
- Later: forced casual sexual encounters and hallucinogen use
- Anti-Semitic propaganda and Hitler praise (describing Germans as the Aryan master race)

### III. THE MACHINE: RECRUITMENT & EXPLOITATION

**Recruitment Method:** "Bring me the beauties." Von Mierers directed followers to recruit attractive, wealthy, accomplished young people — primarily from fashion, Ivy League schools, and Manhattan social circles. He met many at beaches (notably Nantucket, where he encountered 16-year-old Hoyt Richards in 1981).

**Key Victims:**
- **Hoyt Richards** — Ford model, became the world's top male supermodel, gave the cult millions over ~15 years
- **Jacki Adams** — Ford model, whistleblower, later contacted Manhattan DA about gem scams
- **Richard & Robert Dupont** — Dupont twins, Warhol Factory fixtures, recruited at age 16
- **Susanne Gregard** — model charged $50,000+ for gems

**Living Conditions:** Inner circle members (up to ~100 at peak) slept on futons on the floor of von Mierers' East 54th Street apartment. All possessions fit in a 3×3 foot closet space. Members were forbidden from falling in love, seeing family, or having outside friends.

**Business Empire — "UFO" (Ultimate Fitness Opportunities):**
- Psychic astrology reading tapes: $350 each
- "Gem prescriptions" — gemstones with fraudulent appraisals
- Books, videos, cassettes, supplements
- Exercise equipment
- Public access TV show and radio program
- Mailing list of 40,000+ people

**The Gem Scam:** Von Mierers sold nearly $2 million worth of gemstones using inflated appraisals from jewelers on West 47th Street. He told followers the stones were "God's thoughts condensed into crystal form." Jacki Adams was charged upward of $100,000. One follower paid $17,000 for a sapphire, topaz, and cat's-eye that were not worth wholesale prices.

**Sexual Hypocrisy:** While demanding celibacy from followers, von Mierers (who was gay but claimed to be asexual) paid male escorts several times a week. Assistant Paul Hinton recalled being instructed to drive to Times Square to find "the biggest piece of trash in the universe" to satisfy him.

### IV. THE VANITY FAIR EXPOSÉ & MANHATTAN DA INVESTIGATION

In March 1990, *Vanity Fair* published Marie Brenner's exposé "East Side Alien" — the definitive contemporary account. The piece went to press before von Mierers' death.

**Key revelations from the article:**
- Manhattan District Attorney's office was actively investigating von Mierers
- Investigation focused on multimillion-dollar gem fraud
- A detective was quoted asking a fleeing model: "How could an obvious phony like this convince so many smart, attractive young people in New York that he was for real?"
- The article estimated ~$2 million in fraudulent gem sales

**Von Mierers' response to the investigation:** He fled New York and retreated to the Eternal Values compound at Lake Lure, North Carolina.

The Manhattan DA dropped the investigation after von Mierers died, discovering his real name was Freddie Miers and that he had been raised Jewish in Brooklyn.

---

### V. DEATH & AFTERMATH

Von Mierers died February 4, 1990, at age 43, "surrounded by a pink marble interior and numerous mirrors" at the Lake Lure compound. The 3.38-acre lakefront property had been purchased in 1987 for $400,000 — largely funded by Hoyt Richards' modeling income.

He concealed his AIDS diagnosis from all followers except assistant Paul Hinton. Some members believed he was murdered — specifically that Eternal Values member Fritz Steven suffocated him with a pillow as a mercy killing. These allegations have never been confirmed.

**Post-Death Timeline:**
- **1990:** Group splits into two factions. One faction retreats to North Carolina, stockpiling guns, gold, and food.
- **1990–1999:** Group becomes increasingly extreme and abusive under new leadership. Richards is interrogated for hours about "ego lapses." New leaders tell him: "I just wish you had the guts to kill yourself."
- **July 3, 1999:** Richards escapes, calling Fabio (the romance cover model), who buys him a plane ticket to Los Angeles, lets him stay for 18 months, and gives him the keys to a Porsche.
- **2002:** Richards' lawyers settle with remaining group members, liquidate Eternal Values' assets. The organization effectively ceases to exist.

### VII. WHAT HBO LEFT OUT OR SOFTENED

1. **The anti-Semitism:** Multiple sources confirm von Mierers spewed anti-Semitic propaganda and praised Hitler — notable given his own Jewish upbringing. HBO's docuseries reportedly minimizes this.

2. **The sexual abuse of minors:** Richard Dupont was 16 when von Mierers lured him to his apartment, instructed him to remove his jeans, and left him in boxer shorts. Dupont wondered for years if he had been raped. This pattern of targeting teenagers is underexplored.

3. **The murder allegations:** The pillow suffocation claim by follower Fritz Steven is mentioned in HBO's series but presented as unconfirmed rumor rather than a credible allegation requiring investigation.

4. **The full scope of gem fraud:** While HBO mentions the gem scam, the $2 million figure and the active Manhattan DA criminal investigation are underemphasized compared to the spiritual narrative.

5. **Post-death extremism:** The North Carolina faction's stockpiling of weapons, the interrogation sessions, and the psychological torture of Richards after von Mierers' death receive limited treatment.

6. **The 2002 lawsuit:** Richards' civil action that liquidated Eternal Values and recovered funds is mentioned but not explored as a legal precedent.

### VIII. CONNECTION TO NXIVM (THERE IS NONE)

Frederick von Mierers and Eternal Values have **no documented connection** to Keith Raniere, NXIVM, or Consumers' Buyline. They are separate cults operating in different decades with different ideologies:

| | **Von Mierers / Eternal Values** | **Raniere / NXIVM** |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | 1978–1999 | 1990s–2018 |
| **Ideology** | New Age / UFO / Arcturian walk-ins | "Rational Inquiry" / Executive Success |
| **Recruitment** | Models, socialites, beach encounters | MLM structure, wealthy elites, actors |
| **Leader Fate** | Died of AIDS 1990 | Convicted 2019, sentenced 120 years |
| **HBO Series** | *Bring Me the Beauties* (2026) | *The Vow* (2020–2022) |

The confusion likely stems from HBO producing both documentaries and both involving charismatic leaders exploiting wealthy, attractive followers in New York-adjacent environments.
